HOW SOMBRA CALCULATES A ROUTE

Street by street, shadow by shadow.

  1. 1
    Load the walkable network

    Pedestrian-accessible OpenStreetMap streets are converted into a local routing graph.

  2. 2
    Read real building heights

    Official Madrid footprints and metre heights are loaded for the route corridor. If that service is unreachable, OpenStreetMap heights are used instead and the result is labelled as estimated.

  3. 3
    Project the sun

    Solar altitude and bearing are calculated for the chosen date and Madrid time. Every footprint is projected away from the sun according to its height.

  4. 4
    Score every street

    Sample points along each street segment estimate its shaded percentage. Dijkstra’s algorithm then balances distance against sun exposure, and the drawn route is coloured yellow where the sun hits and brown where a building shades it.

Pilot limitation: shadows are geometric estimates. Trees, awnings, clouds, construction and temporary closures are not yet modelled. Always follow real street signs.
